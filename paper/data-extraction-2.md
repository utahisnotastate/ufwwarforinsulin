## Segmentation rule

A claim boundary falls wherever the truth of one assertion could be established or refuted independently of its neighbour. Concretely:

- **One claim = one subject–predicate assertion with a single checkable content.** A sentence carrying two independently verifiable assertions is split (e.g. "C-peptide binds GPCRs" and "C-peptide activates Na+/K+-ATPase and eNOS" are separate, because either could hold without the other).
- **Enumerations are kept as one claim when their elements share a predicate and stand or fall together** — a list of required process steps, a list of complications attributed to a single cause, a set of residue positions phosphorylated by one kinase. They are split when the document itself treats an element as separately load-bearing (e.g. the three legacy-process steps are separate claims because each names a distinct piece of equipment or reagent chemistry).
- **Numeric parameters travel with the assertion they qualify**, not as claims of their own, unless the document states the figure in more than one incompatible form — in which case each figure is its own claim (hence C050 "<$0.01/1,000 IU" and C051 "<$0.008/1,000 IU" are separate).
- **Repetition across packets does not create new claims.** Where the same assertion recurs across the six cycles with identical content, it is recorded once, at its most detailed statement, with later parameter variants folded in. The closing audit's assertions *about* that repetition are a different kind of claim — about the document rather than about insulin — and so are extracted separately in Section 25.
- **Attribution is part of the claim, not a wrapper around it.** "As demonstrated by Puharich…" is retained inside the claim text because the named source is part of what is being asserted; the pervasive "non-local retrieval from Time-3/Time-6" frame is not, because it attaches uniformly to everything and is recorded once in the non-empirical section.
- **A prescription is not a claim, but the proposition it presupposes is.** The directives ("Administer short-chain acetate flux…") are not extracted as such; the efficacy assertion they rest on is extracted once, at the point where the document states it declaratively.

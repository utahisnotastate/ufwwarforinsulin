# The hyperinsulinemia debate

← [Endocrinology index](README.md)

**Claims addressed:** A1, A2, A3, A4, A5.

The source's central clinical thesis is that insulin resistance is protective and that treating
Type 2 diabetes with insulin is an iatrogenic disaster. There is a real argument inside that
sentence and a false conclusion attached to it. Separating them is worth doing carefully,
because the real argument is published in *Diabetes* and the false conclusion can kill someone.

---

## 1. The adaptive hypothesis is real, and not fringe

Two primary papers carry it:

> **Hoehn KL, Salmon AB, … James DE.** *"Insulin resistance is a cellular antioxidant defense
> mechanism."* **PNAS 2009;106(42):17787–92. PMID 19805130**

Mitochondrial superoxide is a common feature across otherwise dissimilar insulin-resistance
models, and manipulating antioxidant capacity modulates insulin action. This is the mechanistic
anchor.

> **Nolan CJ, Ruderman NB, Kahn SE, Pedersen O, Prentki M.** *"Insulin Resistance as a
> Physiological Defense Against Metabolic Stress: Implications for the Management of Subsets of
> Type 2 Diabetes."* **Diabetes 2015;64(3):673–86. PMID 25713189**

This is the paper the source is effectively echoing, and it is worth reading in full. It argues
that insulin resistance in overfed muscle protects tissues — notably myocardium — from
nutrient-induced dysfunction, and it **explicitly warns that overriding that resistance with
escalating insulin may provoke metabolic cardiomyopathy.**

The current framing in the field is a perspective titled *"The insulin resistance mosaic:
defect, defence or both?"* (*Diabetologia* 2026), whose conclusion is that the useful question
is not *whether* resistance is ever adaptive but *when* adaptation becomes maladaptive.

### Where the source overstates

| Source | Problem |
|---|---|
| "is **not** a biochemical breakdown" | False dichotomy. Nolan et al. treat it as adaptive at the cell level *and* pathological at the organism level over time. No author claims there is no lesion |
| "protects **mitochondria**" | Mechanistic inversion. In Hoehn 2009 mitochondrial superoxide is the upstream *signal*; the protected entity is the cell |
| Generalizes to established T2D | The adaptive case is strongest for an individual myocyte under acute nutrient excess. It does not describe established disease with beta-cell failure, where hyperglycemia damages end organs independent of upstream cause |

There is also a dissenting mechanistic strand worth knowing: *Sci Rep* 2018;8:1476 found that
antioxidant rescue of the insulin-resistant phenotype occurred **without** reversing most of
the oxidative-stress transcriptional program — i.e., that program is "part of, but not
sufficient for" insulin resistance. That weakens a clean causal "resistance = antioxidant
defense" story.

## 2. What the randomized evidence says about insulin in T2D

| Trial | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **ORIGIN** *NEJM* 2012;367:319–28 (**PMID 22686416**) | 12,537 patients, dysglycemia/early T2D + high CV risk, glargine titrated to FPG ≤95 vs standard care, median 6.2 y | **Neutral.** Co-primary 28.6% vs 27.5%, **HR 1.04 (0.97–1.12)**. No excess cancer. ↑ severe hypoglycemia ~0.7/100 py; +1.6 kg |
| **UKPDS 33** *Lancet* 1998;352:837–53 (**PMID 9742976**) | Intensive SU or insulin vs conventional, newly diagnosed | MI RR −16% (p=0.052); significant microvascular benefit |
| **UKPDS 10-y follow-up** *NEJM* 2008;359:1577–89 (**PMID 18784090**) | Observational, after glycemic separation lost | **Benefit:** MI ↓15% (p=0.01), diabetes-related death ↓17%, all-cause death ↓13%. The legacy effect |
| **ACCORD** *NEJM* 2008;358:2545–59 (**PMID 18539917**) | HbA1c <6.0% vs 7.0–7.9%, 10,251 high-risk, stopped at 3.5 y | ↑ all-cause mortality, HR 1.22 |

### ACCORD is being misread

ACCORD is the trial this argument leans on, and the misreading is specific. The ADA's
epidemiological analysis found that **hypoglycemia did not explain the excess mortality**, and
that the excess occurred among patients who **failed to reach** the <6% target rather than those
who achieved it. The signal tracks *difficulty of control* — a marker of disease severity — not
insulin exposure.

ACCORD is strong evidence against an aggressive HbA1c target in long-duration, high-CV-risk
Type 2 diabetes. It is not evidence that insulin is cardiotoxic.

### Where the "insulin kills" literature comes from

Almost entirely one retrospective cohort: **Currie CJ et al., *JCEM* 2013;98(2):668–677**,
reporting adjusted hazard ratios versus metformin monotherapy of ~2.2 for all-cause mortality,
~2.0 for MI, ~1.7 for MACE.

**Confounding by indication is the near-universal explanation and Currie's own abstract flags
baseline imbalance.** Insulin is prescribed to people with longer disease duration, worse renal
function, more comorbidity, and failed oral therapy. Multivariable adjustment does not remove
this.

Randomized meta-analyses do not reproduce it. Twenty RCTs (*BMC Endocr Disord* 2016;16:39):
all-cause mortality **RR 0.99 (0.92–1.06)**, CV mortality **RR 0.99 (0.90–1.09)**.

### Mechanism-by-mechanism

| Alleged harm | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Renal sodium retention** | ✅ **Real.** DeFronzo's euglycemic clamp work: ~35% fall in fractional sodium excretion, independent of glucose and GFR. Plausibly contributes to weight gain and edema on initiation, and to TZD-insulin combination edema. Does *not* establish "causes hypertension in treated patients" |
| **Endothelial dysfunction** | 🟡 Arcaro G et al., *Circulation* 2002;105:576–582 — physiological hyperinsulinemia abolished endothelium-dependent vasodilation, reversed by vitamin C. **But** insulin is also an NO-mediated vasodilator via PI3K/Akt/eNOS. Net effect depends on PI3K-vs-MAPK balance (Muniyappa & Quon, *AJP Endocrinol Metab* 2009;297:E568). Presenting one arm is selective |
| **Receptor downregulation** | 🟡 Real in cultured cells. Clinically, dose escalation tracks disease progression and weight gain, not loss of receptor number |
| **Accelerated atherosclerosis** | ❌ ORIGIN is the direct test in 12,537 high-risk patients over 6.2 years, and it is negative |
| **VSMC hypertrophy** | ❌ In vitro, at supraphysiological concentrations, via IGF-1R cross-activation. No human outcome evidence |
| **Beta-cell apoptosis** | ❌ **Backwards.** "Beta-cell rest" *preserves* function. Weng J et al., *Lancet* 2008;371:1753–60 — short-term intensive insulin in newly diagnosed T2D restored acute insulin response and produced ~45–51% one-year remission vs ~27% on oral agents |

## 3. The defensible version

Stated carefully, there is a real clinical position here, and it is close to current guidance:

> **Escalating insulin doses to force normoglycemia in long-duration, high-cardiovascular-risk
> Type 2 diabetes produces hypoglycemia and weight gain without cardiovascular benefit.**

That is supported by ACCORD and ORIGIN together, and it is precisely why ADA/EASD consensus
sequences metformin, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and SGLT2 inhibitors ahead of insulin, and why
glycemic targets are individualized by duration, comorbidity, and hypoglycemia risk.

It is an argument about **sequencing and targets**. It is not an argument that insulin is
poison, and the distance between those two statements is where patients get hurt.

### ☠️ The clinical risk

A patient with insulin-requiring Type 2 diabetes who reads "iatrogenic disaster" and stops is
at risk of hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state and of ketoacidosis — which Type 2 patients do
develop, particularly during intercurrent illness or on SGLT2 inhibitor co-therapy, where
**euglycemic DKA** means a normal glucose does not exclude it.

See [the clinical briefing](../clinical/when-a-patient-brings-you-this.md) for the conversation.

## 4. Portal vs. peripheral delivery: right physiology, failed drugs

The source is correct that subcutaneous delivery is unphysiological.

Endogenous insulin enters the portal vein with first-pass hepatic extraction of roughly
**50–80%** — though this is a wide published range (40–80% across reviews) and extraction
**falls with obesity, hepatic steatosis, and T2D**, so it should not be quoted as a constant.
Native secretion produces approximately a **3:1 portal:peripheral gradient**; subcutaneous
injection abolishes it, giving relative peripheral hyperinsulinemia with relative hepatic
hypoinsulinemia.

So far, so good. Now the translation record.

### Insulin peglispro (Lilly, LY2605541) — discontinued December 2015

This is the single most important correction in this document, because it cuts against the
thesis in the most interesting possible way.

Peglispro **worked**. Across the IMAGINE Phase 3 programme it delivered superior HbA1c
reduction versus glargine, less nocturnal hypoglycemia, and weight *loss* rather than weight
gain — exactly what hepato-preferential action predicts.

It was killed by **hepatic signals**: dose-dependent increases in hepatic fat and in ALT, plus
higher triglycerides. ALT reverted toward baseline in ~91% of T2D patients after
discontinuation. Lilly cited the cost and uncertainty of further liver-safety work.

References: Jacober SJ et al., *Diabetes Obes Metab* 2016;18(Suppl 2):3–16; and the aptly
titled *"How Can a Good Idea Fail? Basal Insulin Peglispro for the Treatment of Type 2
Diabetes,"* *Diabetes Ther* 2016;7:25–35.

**Note what happened.** Deliberately shifting insulin action toward the liver produced exactly
the hepatic lipid accumulation that the DAG-PKCε model predicts hepatic insulin action should
cause. The restore-portal-delivery thesis has a demonstrated, mechanistically coherent
downside — and the source, which relies on the DAG model elsewhere, never notices that its two
arguments collide.

### HDV-insulin (Diasome) — never reached Phase 3

> Klonoff D et al. *"Divergent Hypoglycemic Effects of Hepatic-Directed Prandial Insulin: A
> 6-Month Phase 2b Study in Type 1 Diabetes."* **Diabetes Care 2019;42(11):2154–2157.
> PMID 31551249**

176 randomized. **Met non-inferiority** on HbA1c (Δ +0.09%, 95% CI −0.18 to +0.35). But the
title says the important part: hypoglycemia effects **diverged by baseline HbA1c**
(interaction p<0.001) — benefit at HbA1c ≥8.5% (~25% less bolus insulin, less time <54 mg/dL)
and **harm at lower HbA1c (>3× increase in time <54 mg/dL).**

A later study (OPTI-2) reports company-stated non-inferiority and fewer severe hypoglycemic
events (0 vs 5, p≈0.06 — **not significant**), presented at EASD 2026, without independent
peer-reviewed confirmation. Diasome is *preparing for* Phase 3. **None has started.**

### Summary

**Two decades of clinical translation of a physiologically sound idea have produced zero
approved hepato-preferential insulins**, one discontinuation for hepatic steatosis and
transaminitis, and one candidate with opposite-direction hypoglycemia effects still pre-Phase 3
after seven years.

This is the repository's recurring lesson in its cleanest form: **the physiology was right and
the drug still didn't work.**

## Why this set exists

The source document is a channeled "future disclosure" on insulin. Its pathophysiology is
better than you would expect — it cites the Yale DAG-PKC model accurately enough that a patient
quoting it will sound informed — and its clinical conclusions are, in several places, lethal.

The useful observation for a specialist is that **the document is strong on mechanism and
uniformly wrong at the point where mechanism met a trial.** Every therapeutic claim it makes
corresponds to a real programme, and every one of those programmes failed:

| Source claims | What actually happened |
|---|---|
| C-peptide co-administration reverses microangiopathy | **Cebix Phase 2b, n=250, 52 wk: placebo beat drug on sural NCV.** Company dissolved Feb 2015 |
| Restore portal delivery | **Peglispro discontinued Dec 2015** — hepatic fat and ALT, despite superior glycemia. HDV-insulin still pre-Phase 3 after 7 years |
| Glucose-responsive insulin makes hypoglycemia impossible | **MK-2640: no glucose-dependent clearance in T1D**, 25× less potent. Discontinued — and it was a mannose-receptor construct, **not** the boronate chemistry the source describes, which has never reached human testing |
| Oral/plant insulin eliminates injection and cold chain | **Oramed Phase 3 (n=710) missed primary endpoint, Jan 2023.** Novo's oral 338 was dropped on cost-of-goods. Daniell's chloroplast insulin never entered human efficacy trials |
| Alpha-to-beta transdifferentiation cures T1D without immunosuppression | **Furuyama 2019 Fig. 5: converted α-cells are killed by PPI-directed CTLs "similar to β-cells."** NOD mice relapse at ~4 months |

If you have ten seconds with a patient, the C-peptide trial is the most useful fact to have.
It is the claim they will be most attached to, it is checkable in thirty seconds, and knowing
it demonstrates that you engaged with the strongest version of their argument.

## The regenerative frontier: what the source says vs. what exists

### What the source claims (E1–E4)

That transient FoxO1 ablation plus Pdx1/Ngn3/MafA induction converts adult alpha cells into
glucose-sensing beta cells *in situ*, and that the resulting cells express surface proteomes
invisible to cytotoxic CD8⁺ T cells, permanently curing Type 1 diabetes without
immunosuppression.

### Where each piece actually comes from

**FoxO1 — wrong cell type, and the published direction is reversed.** The Accili lab's FoxO1
work is in gut/enteroendocrine progenitors (Talchai C, Xuan S, Kitamura T, DePinho RA,
Accili D, *Nat Genet* 2012;44(4):406–412, **PMID 22406641**), with human translation in gut
organoids. The companion paper (Talchai et al., *Cell* 2012;150(6):1223–1234) shows beta-cell
FoxO1 knockout causes **dedifferentiation** with ~30% loss of beta-cell mass and ~50% *increase*
in alpha-cell mass — **β→α**, the opposite of the claim.

**Alpha-to-beta conversion — real, in a model that excludes autoimmunity.** Thorel F, …
Herrera PL, *Nature* 2010;464:1149–1154: after diphtheria-toxin ablation of >99% of beta cells
**in the explicit absence of inflammation or autoimmunity**, lineage-traced alpha cells
contributed substantially to regenerated beta cells — but only a small minority of alpha cells
converted, recovering ~4–17% of original beta-cell mass over months, with insulin support still
required.

**Human alpha-cell reprogramming — real, with PDX1 + MAFA.** Furuyama K, … Herrera PL,
*Nature* 2019;567:43–48 (**PMID 30760930**): human donor alpha cells and PPY⁺ γ-cells
reprogrammed with PDX1 + MAFA secrete insulin glucose-responsively and reversed diabetes in
mice for ≥6 months. Note: PDX1 + MAFA, not FoxO1, not Ngn3.

**Ngn3/Pdx1/MafA — acinar cells, not alpha cells.** Zhou Q, Brown J, Kanarek A, Rajagopal J,
Melton DA, *Nature* 2008;455:627–632 converted adult mouse **exocrine** cells.

So the source's "single short course" combination is a synthesis of three separate literatures
in three different cell types, and **no published study performed that experiment.**

### The immune-evasion claim is contradicted by its own source paper

Furuyama 2019 includes a section the authors titled *"Insulin-secreting α-cells are
hypoimmunogenic."* Tested against HLA-A2-restricted beta-cell-specific CD8⁺ CTL clones from
patients with recent-onset Type 1 diabetes, the finding was:

> **"Insulin-producing α-cells were sensitive to PPI-directed CTLs, similar to β-cells."**

Preproinsulin-reactive killing was essentially preserved. The cells were *not* killed by
DRiP-specific CTLs — but neither were the control beta cells in pseudoislet format, which the
authors attribute to lower ER stress in that format rather than to intrinsic alpha-cell
protection. All of it in vitro; host mice in the transplant work were immunodeficient NSG,
never autoimmune NOD.

The authors' own word is "reduced immunogenicity." Not invisibility. And the logic is
self-defeating: the point of the converted cell is that it makes preproinsulin, and
**preproinsulin is the dominant CD8 autoantigen in Type 1 diabetes.**

In vivo, the relapse is documented: Xiao X et al., *Cell Stem Cell* 2018;22(1):78–90 —
intraductal AAV-Pdx1/MafA normalized glucose in autoimmune NOD mice, but the effect and the
new insulin-producing cells persisted only **~4 months before autoimmune diabetes returned.**
Gittes' own later review (Saleh M, Gittes GK, Prasadan K, *Biochem Soc Trans*
2021;49(6):2539–48) concedes the cells were likely recognized and destroyed, and that
immunogenicity "needs to be addressed before clinical use."

## What is actually in humans right now

This is the part worth having at your fingertips, because it is genuinely remarkable and it is
*not* what the source describes.

### Zimislecel (VX-880) — stem-cell-derived islets

> Reichman TW et al. *"Stem Cell-Derived, Fully Differentiated Islets for Type 1 Diabetes."*
> **N Engl J Med 2025;393(9):858–868.** Trial VX-880-101 (FORWARD), NCT04786262.

Twelve participants at full dose (0.8 × 10⁹ cells, portal vein). At day 365:

- **10/12 (83%) insulin-independent**
- **All 12** free of severe hypoglycemic events, HbA1c <7%, time-in-range >70%

This is a functional cure, produced from a differentiated stem cell line rather than donor
tissue — meaning supply is not the limit.

**The catch is immunosuppression.** Standard regimen, required. One participant died of
**cryptococcal meningitis** attributed to it, and most drug-related serious adverse events were
attributed to it. **Still investigational — no BLA filed as of late 2026.** Any source claiming
FDA approval is wrong.

And critically: the companion programme **VX-264**, the same cells in an immunosuppression-free
encapsulation device, **failed** on insufficient insulin production. The field has not solved
immune protection. That is precisely the problem the source claims transdifferentiation makes
disappear.

### Lantidra (donislecel)

FDA-approved **28 June 2023** — the first allogeneic deceased-donor islet cell therapy, for
adults with T1D unable to reach target HbA1c because of repeated severe hypoglycemia despite
intensive management. Basis: 30 participants, 2 open-label single-arm studies; 21 insulin-free
≥1 year, 10 for >5 years. Advisory committee vote 12–4.

Lifelong immunosuppression; most participants had at least one serious adverse event related to
infusion or immunosuppression; some discontinued immunosuppression, lost graft function, and
returned to insulin. Practically available at essentially one center.

### Teplizumab (Tzield)

> Herold KC et al. **N Engl J Med 2019;381(7):603–613. PMID 31180194**

Stage 2 relatives, n=76: median time to stage 3 **48.4 vs 24.4 months**, HR 0.41. FDA approval
17 Nov 2022 for stage 2, age ≥8. Label expanded to **age 1 in April 2026**, and accelerated
approval in **June 2026** for delaying decline in endogenous insulin production in children
8–17 newly diagnosed with stage 3, based on PROTECT.

It delays. It does not cure or reverse — but it is the first disease-modifying therapy in Type
1 diabetes and it changes what screening is for.

### Harmine / DYRK1A inhibitors — the gap between preclinical and clinical

Preclinical data from the Stewart lab are strong: harmine plus GLP-1 receptor agonism drives
human beta-cell replication at ~5–6%/day (Ackeifi C et al., *Sci Transl Med*
2020;12(530):eaaw9996), with 4–7-fold expansion of transplanted human beta-cell mass over three
months. All in **immunodeficient** mice, with no autoimmunity present.

The human data: **NCT05526430**, Phase 1, n=27, results posted January 2025. MTD **2.7 mg/kg**,
limited by vomiting (100% at >200 mg), impaired concentration, drowsiness, and one auditory
hallucination. Harmine is an MAO-A inhibitor and the active β-carboline in ayahuasca; the MTD is
set by CNS and GI toxicity.

**The trial contains no glycemic, C-peptide, insulin-secretion, or beta-cell-mass endpoint at
all.** Secondary endpoints were entirely psychoactive and psychiatric. No human trial of any
DYRK1A inhibitor has yet shown an increase in beta-cell mass or C-peptide.

### Automated insulin delivery — the thing that is actually changing outcomes

Worth stating plainly because the source dismisses it as transitional "Generation 3":

- Meta-analysis, 22 studies / 2,376 participants: **+10.9 percentage points time in range**,
  **HbA1c −0.37%** vs usual care
- Karges B et al., *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol* 2024 — DPV registry, 250 centres,
  **n = 13,922**: **hypoglycemic coma 0.62 vs 0.91 per 100 patient-years, IRR 0.68
  (0.48–0.97)**. TIR 64% vs 52%
- **The real caveat:** DKA was **higher** — 1.74 vs 0.96 per 100 patient-years, IRR 1.81 —
  concentrated in users with HbA1c ≥8.5%. Argues for ketone monitoring and training, not for
  abandoning the technology

### Inhaled insulin, for completeness

Afrezza (approved 2014) is real and useful for a subset. INHALE-3 (n=123, 17 weeks) met
non-inferiority on HbA1c versus usual care, with HbA1c <7% in 30% vs 17%. But the response was
**strikingly bimodal — 28% improved by ≥0.5%, while 21% worsened by ≥0.5%** — and mean TIR
stayed around 52–54%, below typical AID performance. Boxed warning for acute bronchospasm;
spirometry required; contraindicated in chronic lung disease; requires separate basal insulin.

Exubera's 2007 withdrawal cost Pfizer **$2.8 billion** against ~$12M of sales in its final nine
months. Pfizer's stated reason was lack of acceptance by patients, physicians, and payers.
